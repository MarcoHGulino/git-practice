https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/articles/how-to-modernise-a-live-game-without-breaking-its-history/

This article delves into the multiple variables of updates to live service games. As someone who loves to play many games and sometimes keeps up with patch notes/fixes, it was really interesting to read about how those decisions are made. When a team wants to make an update to existing code, they have to consider how that might affect other systems in the game, how it will affect the devices the game is played on, performance-wise, and how to future-proof these additions. On top of this, the devs need to understand what the player feedback tells them, and be able to communicate with each other easily concerning the code. The article proposes a method to keep track of this shared code, by introducing clear standards, automatic testing, and the distinguishing of legacy code and possibly volatile code. I've been interested in this field for a while and learning about the inner-workings of the engineering team introduced me to a multitude of factors I didn't know much about.
